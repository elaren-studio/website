A practice website needs to help someone understand the services, meet the people behind them, and find a next step. This case study examines how the [Wright Wellness website](https://www.wrightwellness.me/) organizes those decisions.

**Our relationship:** Elaren Studio is credited as the site's designer. Felix Torres, Elaren's Director, also serves as Wright Wellness's Operations Manager. This is a project with a direct working relationship, not an independent client testimonial. The work described here concerns website design, content presentation, and implementation; it does not establish clinical authorship or clinical outcomes.

## The brief in practice

Wright Wellness is a mental health and wellness practice in Arlington, Texas. Its website connects several services with individual team profiles and an appointment-request route. The design challenge is to provide that range of information while keeping the first visit approachable.

## Before and after: documented changes

The comparison below uses retained website source history and the current public page. It is not a comparison of measured conversion rates. The screenshots show the current site; no verified screenshot of the earlier live homepage was available for this review.

<div class="overflow-x-auto" role="region" aria-label="Website changes comparison" tabIndex={0}>

| Element | Earlier implementation | Current implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage introduction | A longer welcome heading, reflection, and appointment, phone, and Spanish links appeared together in the hero. | A short welcome heading and separate reflection introduce the practice, with an appointment-request button in the header. |
| Service navigation | A grid of service cards included descriptions and individual service links. | A compact service list links to dedicated pages, with an overview and a separate starting-point link. |
| Choosing a next step | The service overview link existed on its own. | A June 2026 update added guidance for visitors who are unsure which service to choose. |
| Team browsing | An earlier team preview used an automatically scrolling presentation. | The team preview has arrow controls and touch scrolling, with links to individual profiles. |

</div>

Both earlier and current versions have headings, service information, and contact paths. The change is in their organization and presentation. It would be inaccurate to describe the earlier site as lacking an H1 or having no useful service structure.

## A specific writing change

The service section now tells visitors:

> “You do not need to know the right service before reaching out”

That sentence addresses a concrete uncertainty: a visitor may be ready to ask for help without knowing which service label fits. The adjacent link leads to the starting-point explanation on the services overview. This is an example of connecting copy to a useful destination, rather than adding another general contact prompt.

## Implementation details that support the experience

The retained change history documents the February 2026 homepage restructuring, March team-carousel controls, and June starting-point guidance. Team images include names as alternative text, explicit image dimensions, and lazy loading. These are verifiable implementation details, not a complete accessibility certification or a measured speed improvement.

The short “Welcome” heading is a design choice with a tradeoff: it communicates tone but does not explain the practice's services or location by itself. Service pages, navigation, and contextual copy must carry that information. A next iteration could test a more descriptive introduction while preserving the practice's voice.
